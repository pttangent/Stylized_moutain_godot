## Build sequence

### 1. Lay out the player route first

First trace visible paths and stair runs in **source-image pixels** into a versioned `analysis/p1_blockout_<date>/image_route_trace.json`. Give each segment a stable ID and mark it `visible`, `partly_occluded` or `inferred`; preserve pixel points so the trace can be drawn back over the original. Then create a route skeleton from `navigation_graph.json`: lower-right start → bridge → left-bank ascent → upper-left terrace, plus the right-bank branch to the upper-right terrace. Mark bridge landings, stair flights, turns, rest platforms and blocked edges. Check plan and elevation continuity before adding large terrain masses. The graph's straight lines are **connections, not final path centerlines**. Follow traced paths where visible; use short plausible connections only for occluded sections and label them inferred. Keep new waypoints and edge changes in a versioned P1 route file; never move or rewrite P0 anchors in place.

Do not turn a source pixel into a metric 3D point by choosing an assumed height and back-projecting it through the candidate PnP camera. Such a point is a hypothesis only. Do not treat the first GLB ray hit at a path pixel as a road surface: retain ordered hits where possible and classify them against the image, surface orientation and neighboring geometry. A hit on canopy, cliff, water or background is not a confirmed path point. If camera fit or a ray hit is ambiguous, preserve the pixel trace and represent depth/height as a range or `UNKNOWN` until blockout design resolves it.

### 2. Build reference-matched terrain around the routes

Block out broad terraces, river corridor, rock walls, pools and stepped waterfalls with simple editable forms. Keep the major image landmarks visible from a source-matching review camera: distant falls, middle cascade, main waterfall and pool, small wooden bridge, foreground stream, left stair/terrace, and right stair/terrace. Preserve their depth order and approximate screen positions before refining local dimensions.

The current heightmap provides a bulk-terrain baseline only. **Do not copy its long smooth upstream slope or narrow main-pool trench without review.** The original image shows separated ledges and falls, a broader main pool, irregular banks and cliff faces. Create distinct upstream water levels and waterfall lips; widen and shape the central pool to match the image. Model vertical cliffs, undercuts, bridge deck and stairs as separate geometry. Do not turn tree crowns, water surfaces or background shells into walkable ground.

### 3. Fit paths, stairs, bridge and water to the terrain

Give paths walkable width and continuous supports. Replace any route segment that cuts through a cliff, floats above ground, crosses water without a bridge or hides an unexplained vertical jump with bends, platforms, switchbacks or stairs. Pay particular attention to the long right-upper ascent and the left-upper connection; the overlay exposes straight-line shortcuts that are not physical paths. Retain the designed 6 m bridge span as a starting scale, then verify deck width, landings, clearance above water and railing height against the 1.75 m P0 character.

Make the water course descend from the distant falls through the main pool and under the bridge to the foreground outlet. Give each visible waterfall an upstream lip, a separate falling face and a lower receiving surface. Keep water and navigation collision separate.

### 4. Implement a playable Godot graybox

Use reusable Blender Python generators and editable `.blend` sources for terrain sections, cliff walls, riverbed, bridge, stairs and paths. Export separate Godot-compatible `.glb` modules; do not merge the canyon into one uneditable mesh. Build upper-canyon, central-waterfall and lower-basin Godot sub-scenes plus a combined scene. Use temporary clay or height-coded materials only.

Add static collision and a **1.75 m** `CharacterBody3D` test player with the P0 radius, gravity and basic movement. A hidden smooth ramp may support visible stairs, but its reach and clearance must match the visible flight. Build navigation data or explicit route probes and traverse the main and right-bank routes. Use the P0 orthographic camera for game readability, a free engineering camera for geometry inspection, and a separate source-matching camera for image comparison.

Keep raw GLB units, authored Godot design meters and camera-inferred depth/height hypotheses distinct in code and reports. Apply each coordinate transform exactly once and label bounds with units. Do not interpret a large transformed collider bounding box as an import bug until raw bounds and applied scale have been checked. Preserve P0 nodes and parameters; put route revisions in a separate P1 file with a change log and reasons.
